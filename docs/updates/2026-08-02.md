# Literature and Artifact Audit — 2026-08-02

## Scope

This update ran a retrospective omission sweep from the CLIP-era start of foundation-model-based robot evaluation through 2026-08-02. It complements the incremental search in earlier audits: older representative methods were not excluded merely because they predate the newest general-purpose reward models.

Claims were checked against original papers, official proceedings or OpenReview pages, author project pages, official repositories, and model/data cards. Paper, code, weights, data, venue, and license status remain separate fields.

## Result

Eleven records were added to `registry/papers.yaml`:

### CLIP-era foundations and bridge methods

- **VIP** learns a visual value representation from passive human video and uses representation distance as dense goal-conditioned reward.
- **LIV** learns language-image representations from video and exposes a goal-conditioned visual reward for robotic manipulation.
- **RoboCLIP** uses video-language similarity to a single demonstration as reward for robot policy learning.
- **VLM-RM** uses CLIP-family vision-language representations as zero-shot language-conditioned rewards for robot reinforcement learning.
- **Diffusion Reward** uses negative conditional entropy from a conditional video diffusion model as history-conditioned reward for expert-like behavior.
- **Video-Language Critic** learns a temporal video-language preference critic for robot policy learning.
- **Generative Value Learning (GVL)** predicts completion percentages from trajectory frames and uses the value signal for policy learning.

### Newer omissions found in the same sweep

- **MARVL** fine-tunes a VLM for spatial and semantic consistency and constructs multi-stage, trajectory-sensitive guidance for robot manipulation.
- **MVR** learns multi-view video state relevance and combines it with task reward through state-dependent shaping.
- **Reward as An Agent** actively evaluates physical plausibility and task completion in embodied world-model rollouts to provide robust reward signals.
- **CoRe** combines a VLM-optimized formal reward with a residual reward learned from VLM-labelled video preferences.

The README also adds the 2026 **Progress Reward Modeling for Robotic Learning** survey and the ICML 2026 position paper **Good Embodied Reward Models Need Bad Behavior Data** as field-framing resources. They are not treated as reward-model implementations.

## Metadata and artifact corrections

- **Adapt2Reward** is attributed to its verified ECCV 2024 paper record; its registry identifier was corrected from `ma_2024_adapt2reward` to `yang_2024_adapt2reward`.
- **ReWiND** is updated from preprint-only metadata to its CoRL 2025 PMLR proceedings record.
- **SARM** is updated to its ICLR 2026 OpenReview record.
- **TOPReward** now records the verified paper title, arXiv identifier, official MIT-licensed repository, and the author-reported ManiRewardBench evaluation. No standalone public benchmark-data release was inferred.
- For **RoboCLIP** and **MVR**, upstream S3D or ViCLIP checkpoints are recorded as upstream dependencies rather than method-specific released weights.
- **GVL**, **MARVL**, and **CoRe** retain explicit “not verified” artifact states where no official reusable release was found.
- A public repository is not interpreted as a code, checkpoint, or dataset license unless the applicable license was verified.

## Taxonomy decisions

1. CLIP-era similarity or embedding distance is an evaluator interface, but not automatically a calibrated success probability.
2. VIP, LIV, RoboCLIP, VLM-RM, GVL, and MARVL are placed under `absolute_progress` because their primary interface supplies a task-conditioned scalar or completion-like value for an individual observation or trajectory. Their temporal, similarity, or value-learning construction is preserved through secondary tags.
3. Diffusion Reward and Video-Language Critic are placed under `temporal_value` because history or trajectory evidence is central to the native evaluative quantity.
4. MVR, Reward as An Agent, and CoRe are placed under `hybrid_verifier` because their native interface combines multiple reward components, state relevance, physical/task judgments, or other non-equivalent evaluative signals.
5. Foundation-model provenance, training supervision, inference evidence, and downstream role remain cross-cutting dimensions; they do not replace reward semantics.
6. Older foundation-era works remain core survey entries when they meet the technical scope. Publication recency is not an inclusion criterion.

## Screened but not added to the core registry

- Generic vision-language-action policies and world/action models without an explicit evaluative interface remain outside the core.
- ProgressLM/OpenGVL-style benchmark or diagnostic resources were not added in this bounded paper-registry update; benchmark-registry changes require a separate benchmark evidence audit.
- Broad non-robot reward-model papers were not added solely because they use CLIP or a vision-language model.
- The progress survey and bad-behavior position paper are listed as related framing, not as model records.

## Benchmark implications

The restored historical coverage makes several benchmark distinctions explicit:

- Similarity-to-goal, absolute completion, pairwise preference, and structured verification must not be collapsed into one nominal progress score.
- RoboCLIP and VLM-RM provide important CLIP-era baselines for testing whether apparent task progress follows semantics or only visual/textual similarity.
- VIP and LIV provide representation-distance baselines whose scaling and calibration should be evaluated separately from outcome correctness.
- Diffusion Reward and Video-Language Critic motivate transition- and trajectory-aware tests.
- MVR motivates native multiview evaluation rather than silently reducing all models to a single camera.

No benchmark-registry schema or benchmark result was changed in this update.
